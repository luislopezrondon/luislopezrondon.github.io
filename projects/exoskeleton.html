Overview

EXO is a student team within IEEE at USF building a powered lower-limb exoskeleton that assists the hip and knee for people with an uneven gait. The leg is driven by four CubeMars AK80-9 actuators on a 48V bus, with a Jetson Orin Nano and Teensy microcontrollers handling compute and control.

As Electronics & Circuitry Lead, I'm responsible for the electrical side of the build: the battery, power distribution, the emergency stop, and the PCB. I also teach PCB design to newer members of the team.

Power architecture

Everything runs from a single 48V pack. Each AK80-9 can pull up to 28A at peak, so instead of designing around four actuators at full peak, I'm specifying torque requirements so each one draws closer to 12–15A. That keeps the battery, wiring, and protection requirements realistic for a student budget.

Emergency stop

The e-stop is wired to cut the motor bus only. The DC-DC converters tap off the battery ahead of it, so hitting the e-stop kills power to the actuators immediately while the Jetson and Teensys stay powered and the system doesn't have to reboot.

Staged approach: a software e-stop during testing and development, and a hardware e-stop that physically breaks the motor circuit in the final build.

Battery

The pack needs to deliver 48V with at least 5Ah of capacity and a maximum discharge rating above 100A. After weighing pre-built e-bike and drone packs against building our own, we went with a custom 13S1P Li-ion pack. It's cheaper, more configurable, and takes up less space than a pre-built pack.

PCB plan

We're prototyping with pre-made modules (buck converters, an IMU breakout, and so on) so the system can be tested quickly. Once the design is proven, it moves onto a custom PCB.

What's next

  • Build and test the hardware e-stop
  • Design the custom power and control PCB
