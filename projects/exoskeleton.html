Overview

EXO is a student team within IEEE at USF building a powered lower-limb exoskeleton that assists the hip and knee for people with an uneven gait. The design is unilateral: it powers one leg only.

Since August 2026 I've been the Electrical Systems Lead. I lead an electrical team with three sub-teams and coordinate with the Controls team.

Actuation

Two CubeMars AK80-9 actuators (48V) drive the hip and knee, and the ankle is passive. The assistance envelope is ±15 N·m per joint, based on gait data from OpenSim modeling done by a teammate. The current assist strategy compensates the full required joint torque, up to a 15 N·m cap.

Compute and control

A Jetson Orin Nano handles high-level compute, and Teensy 4.1 microcontrollers handle low-level motor control, with an IMU for sensing. The Jetson talks to the Teensys over USB serial. The motors share a single CAN bus, and I'm evaluating a CubeMars driver board that accepts UART and handles CAN internally.

Power architecture

Everything runs from a single 48V pack. DC-DC converters step 48V down for the Jetson and to 3.3V for the Teensys and logic. From the pack, power splits into two branches:

Emergency stop

The e-stop cuts only the motor bus. The logic branch taps off ahead of it, so the Jetson and Teensys stay powered when the e-stop is hit and the system doesn't have to reboot.

Staged approach: a software e-stop for development and a hardware e-stop for the final build. For the hardware version I favor a MOSFET switch over a mechanical contactor because it's smaller, which matters in a wearable.

Battery

The pack needs to deliver 48V with at least 5Ah of capacity. I chose a custom pack over pre-built e-bike or drone packs for cost, configurability, and size. I'm still evaluating two layouts: a custom 13S1P pack, or a 6S2P pack with a 24→48V boost converter.

To size it, I ran a MATLAB model of battery current, which gave about 10A maximum and 3–4A mean. This corrected an earlier estimate that had wrongly assumed battery current equals motor current.

PCB plan

We're prototyping with pre-made modules (buck converters, an IMU breakout, and so on) so the system can be tested quickly. After testing, the design moves onto a custom PCB.

What's next

  • Decide between the 13S1P and 6S2P battery layouts
  • Bench-test the power system and e-stop
  • Move the proven design onto a custom PCB
