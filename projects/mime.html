Mime — Robotic Hand

A 5-finger robotic hand controlled by a flex-sensor data glove and by computer vision. V1 works on a breadboard, and the custom PCB V2 is in progress.

Overview

Five flex sensors on a glove are read by the ESP32's ADC, which drives five MG996R servos, one per finger, through a PCA9685 I2C servo driver. A second control mode uses OpenCV on a laptop to track a hand and send finger positions to the ESP32 over USB.

V1 → V2

On V1, the flex-sensor connections came loose on the breadboard during testing. V2 solders the glove's flex sensors directly to the PCB, for a more streamlined and reliable design.

V2 PCB

I'm designing V2 in Altium, which I'm learning for this board. An earlier version was done in KiCad around an ESP32-WROOM-32. The V2 design includes:

  • ESP32-S3-WROOM-1U-N8R8 module with native USB and a U.FL antenna connector
  • Flex sensors on GPIO4–8 (ADC1), chosen to avoid strapping and PSRAM pins
  • PCA9685 on I2C (GPIO1 SDA, GPIO2 SCL) with 4.7kΩ pull-ups, at address 0x40
  • USB-C connector, and a reset circuit with an EN pull-up, RC delay, and reset button

Power

The servos are powered from a 6V supply, and an AMS1117 LDO provides the 3.3V logic rail. Earlier revisions used a 3S LiPo with an MP1584EN buck converter. I designed that buck stage, including the feedback, compensation, and frequency-setting components, before simplifying the power design.

A safety lesson

An overvoltage from a bench supply once destroyed an ESP32 and surged back through USB into a lab PC. Since then I've designed with USB isolation, Schottky diodes to prevent back-feed, and VBUS trace modifications.
