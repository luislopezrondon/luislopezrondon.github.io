PID Tremor-Stabilizing Spoon

A handheld spoon that uses PID control to counteract Parkinson's tremors, built on a custom PCB with its own charging and battery protection.

Overview

Tremors can make something as simple as eating difficult. This spoon measures its own motion and runs a PID loop on an ESP32-C3 to drive servos that move the spoon head against the tremor, keeping it steady.

Control loop
MeasureSpoon orientation
ComputePID on ESP32-C3
ActuateServo correction

The proportional term reacts to the current error, the integral term removes slow drift, and the derivative term damps fast motion, which is what makes the spoon feel steady instead of jittery.

Hardware

The board is built around the ESP32-C3-MINI-1-N4. To keep it small, there's no USB data connection on the board: firmware is flashed over UART (TX/RX), and the USB-C port is used only for charging.

Power moved from a 2S LiPo to a single 18650 cell in a Keystone 1043 holder, with a boost converter for the servos. A TP4056 handles charging, and a DW01A + FS8205 pair protects the cell from over-charge, over-discharge, and shorts.

Teaching it

I turned this board into an Altium workshop for IEEE at USF EXO, walking students through designing their own PID Parkinson's spoon.
