PID Spoon + Altium PCB Workshop

A stabilizing spoon for people with Parkinson's tremors, which uses an IMU and PID control to keep the spoon steady. I designed the custom PCB and use it as the teaching example in an Altium workshop I run for IEEE at USF EXO.

Overview

An IMU on the handle measures the hand's motion. A microcontroller runs a PID loop that drives servos to tilt the spoon head the opposite way, keeping it steady while the hand shakes.

My part was the custom PCB that runs the whole device, not the control loop.

The PCB

The board is built around an STM32G431 microcontroller and an LSM6DSV16X IMU. It runs off a 1S LiPo, with a boost converter for the servos and USB-C for charging and flashing firmware.

The battery circuitry uses a TP4056 charger with DW01A and FS8205 battery protection.

Altium workshop

I run an Altium workshop for IEEE at USF EXO and use this board as the teaching example. About 10 members have completed it so far. They learned PCB design basics, the design process, part research, and datasheet reading, and each designed their first board, a 6-axis IMU board.
