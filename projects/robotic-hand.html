Mimetic Robotic Hand

A flex-sensor data glove that controls a five-servo robotic hand in real time, running on a custom PCB with onboard buck regulation from a 3S LiPo.

Overview

Five flex sensors on a glove measure how far each finger bends. An ESP32-S3 reads them on its ADC and sends matching positions to five MG996R servos through a PCA9685 PWM driver, so the robotic hand mirrors the glove in real time.

The project started as breadboard experiments with a flex sensor and a servo and grew into a full custom PCB.

Signal path
Sense5× flex sensors (glove)
ReadESP32-S3 ADC1
DrivePCA9685 over I2C
Actuate5× MG996R servos

Custom PCB

The first board was designed in KiCad around an ESP32-WROOM-32. I'm now rebuilding it in Altium around an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1U-N8R8, which adds native USB over USB-C, and the 1U variant takes an external antenna through a U.FL connector.

Pin planning mattered on the S3. The flex sensors sit on ADC1 (GPIO4–8) so they keep working with Wi-Fi enabled, and they avoid the GPIO3 strapping pin and the PSRAM pins (35–37). The PCA9685 is on I2C (GPIO1 SDA, GPIO2 SCL) with 4.7kΩ pull-ups, at address 0x40. The flex sensors connect through locking JST-PH connectors.

Power

A 3S LiPo (9–12.6V) feeds a buck converter that produces a dedicated 6V rail for the servos. The first revision used an MP1584EN. The redesign moves to a TI TPS56A37 (10A, 4.5–28V input) for much more current headroom with five servos moving at once.

Servo power
Source3S LiPo, 9–12.6V
ConvertTPS56A37 buck
Rail6V servo supply

Lessons learned

An early ESP32 was destroyed by an overvoltage from a bench supply. That pushed me to treat power isolation as a core part of the design. I added Schottky diodes to prevent back-feeding, kept USB separate from the battery supply, and cut traces on the DevKit to split the two.
