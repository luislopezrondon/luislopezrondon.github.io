Overview

I built this to actually understand DSP and FFTs, not just to get a visualizer working. The goal was to understand every line of code. Audio streams from a PC over USB into a Teensy, which runs a 1024-point FFT and groups the bins into 8 logarithmically spaced bands.

Signal chain
InputUSB audio from PC
Analyze1024-point FFT
Group8 log-spaced bands
DisplayILI9341 TFT

Why logarithmic bands

Human hearing is roughly logarithmic: an octave at 100Hz spans far fewer hertz than an octave at 5kHz. Equal-width bands would cram all the bass into one bar, so log-spaced bands look much closer to what you actually hear. A larger FFT (2048 vs 1024) gives more usable bins, but each frame takes longer, which is the classic time-frequency tradeoff.

Debugging the audio chain

At first the chain wouldn't produce usable output. Swapping the FFT for AudioAnalyzePeak proved the audio path itself was fine, which pointed at configuration instead of wiring. The root cause turned out to be AudioMemory(12), which is far too small for the FFT's buffering. Raising it to 60 fixed it.

Next step: profile with AudioMemoryUsageMax() and size the memory pool from real data instead of a guess.

Display & line-in

The output is moving from the Arduino Serial Plotter to an ILI9341 TFT on the Teensy's hardware SPI bus, driven with the ILI9341_t3 library. I also added an aux line-in through the PJRC audio shield.

Next revision: custom PCB

The next version is a custom PCB designed with a teammate. It keeps USB audio in, adds line-in through an ADC circuit I'm designing myself, and adds a DAC output to speakers so the system can play audio as well as display it. I'm evaluating an STM32 for this revision.
